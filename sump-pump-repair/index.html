West Milford receives about 51 inches of precipitation a year, and the Highlands terrain sends runoff downhill into foundations. The township has a flood history that residents remember, including Hurricane Irene in August 2011 when floodwater reached homes in the Wallisch area.

A pump that fails during a storm is the worst timing, which is why we treat sump pump calls as emergencies and plan for backup power.

What goes wrong with a sump pump

The usual failures are a stuck float switch, a clogged intake, a burned-out motor or a check valve that has failed and lets water run back into the pit. A pump that runs constantly, short-cycles or makes grinding noise is warning you.

If the discharge pipe is frozen or blocked outside, the pump runs without moving water, so the plumber follows the water all the way out of the house.

Signs your pump is struggling

Check these before the next storm.

Runs constantly or never runs

A stuck float, a failed switch or a pit that is too small for the inflow.

Loud rattle or grinding

A worn impeller or debris in the pump.

Water remains in the pit

The pump is not lifting what flows in.

Pump is more than several years old

Aging motors fail without warning.

Backup power and battery systems

A storm that floods the basement also tends to knock out the power. A battery backup pump or a water-powered backup keeps the pit from overflowing when the primary pump has no electricity.

If your basement is damp even in dry weather, a sump pump is only part of the answer and plumbing installation and drainage work may be a better fit.

Basement waterproofing is a related question

Waterproofing sections of the basement, such as interior drain tile, coatings or exterior grading, can reduce what the pump has to handle. A pump removes water that has already arrived, while waterproofing slows the arrival.

Ask the plumber what combination fits your foundation and how much water the pit receives in a typical storm.

Sump pump questions

Do you handle sump pump repair emergencies at night or on weekends in West Milford?

Yes. A failed pump during a storm is an emergency and the 24/7 line takes those calls nights, weekends and holidays. Unplug the pump and call.

How often should I test the pump?

Pour a bucket of water into the pit every few months and watch that it starts, empties and shuts off.

How long does a sump pump last?

It varies with use. A pump that runs a lot in a wet area wears out sooner.

Does a battery backup really help?

Yes during outages. Test the battery regularly and replace it on schedule.

Where should the discharge go?

Well away from the foundation, and clear of walkways. The plumber checks the route during the visit.

Is a sump pump required for a basement here?

That depends on the property and the water table. The plumber can tell you whether yours needs one.

What code covers a sump pump installation?

The discharge piping follows the National Standard Plumbing Code under N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and the outlet and circuit follow the National Electrical Code (NEC), which an electrician handles.
