When a tank splits open or stops heating for good, the first move is to shut off the cold water to the heater and the power or fuel, then call our 24/7 line. If water is already on the floor, keep it away from outlets and move anything valuable off the slab while you wait.

We can replace a failed tank the same day when the schedule allows, and the plumber walks you through the choices on site rather than selling you a model over the phone. Fuel type, tank size, venting and the condition of the supply lines all shape what goes in.

Replacement is a permitted trade in West Milford. The township's Department of Building Safety handles plumbing permits through its plumbing subcode, and a licensed plumber pulls that paperwork so the installation is inspected rather than left unrecorded.

Signs a repair is no longer the answer

A tank that leaks from its shell cannot be patched. Rusty hot water, a tank well past the age most heaters last or a string of repairs in two years all point toward replacement.

If you are not sure, a quick look from the plumber settles it. We compare the cost of the repair in front of us with the remaining life of the tank and tell you which makes sense.

What an emergency replacement looks like

The plumber drains and removes the old tank, checks the shutoff valves, supply connectors and venting, and sets the new unit on a proper pan and drain line where the layout allows. The relief valve discharge, expansion needs and gas or electrical connection are all verified before the heater is filled.

Once the tank is up to temperature, the plumber checks for leaks, tests combustion on gas models and shows you the shutoff points. If you want a fresh start on the whole heater area, ask about water heater installation details such as pan, strapping and drain routing.

A note for well-water homes

The township's groundwater study found that private wells are widespread here, and well water can carry sediment and hardness that coat the bottom of a tank. A sediment filter before the heater, regular flushing and anode rod checks help the next tank last longer.

If you are curious whether a tankless unit suits your water, read about tankless water heater installation before you decide, since scale matters more on those.

What goes into the price of a replacement

Replacement quotes vary more than repair quotes, so it helps to know the pieces.

Unit size and fuel type

A larger tank or a different fuel changes both the unit and the connection work.

Venting and gas or electrical work

Some replacements need a new vent liner, gas fitting or circuit.

Code upgrades

Older installations may need a new expansion tank, pan, relief piping or shutoff valves.

Permit and inspection

A permitted job includes the filing and the inspection visit.

Haul-off and site conditions

Tight stairs and long carry-outs add time.

Insurance rarely pays for the heater itself but may respond to water damage from a sudden failure. You get a clear price before work starts, and the exact number comes after one call.

Water heater replacement questions

Do you handle water heater replacement emergencies at night or on weekends in West Milford?

Yes. When a tank fails at night or on a weekend, the 24/7 line is staffed and we schedule the replacement for the earliest same-day slot the schedule allows. Shut off cold water and power or fuel while you wait.

How long does a water heater replacement take?

A straightforward swap of a like-for-like tank usually takes part of a day. A fuel change, new venting or a tankless conversion takes longer.

Do I need a permit in West Milford?

Plumbing work is covered by the plumbing subcode through the township's Department of Building Safety, so ask the plumber to confirm the permit is filed. We handle that as part of a licensed installation.

Can I switch from electric to gas or propane?

Sometimes. It depends on the fuel available at the house, the vent path and local rules. The plumber checks these on a site visit before quoting.

Should I replace the supply connectors too?

Usually yes. Old braided or flexible connectors are inexpensive compared with the risk of a failure right after the new tank goes in.

What happens to the old tank?

The plumber removes it and takes it away so you are not left with a tank in the basement.

What code does a replacement heater have to meet?

The installation follows the National Standard Plumbing Code as adopted by New Jersey in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, plus the manufacturer's instructions, and the township plumbing inspector can review it.
