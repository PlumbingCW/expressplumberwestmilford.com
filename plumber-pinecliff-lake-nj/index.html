Pinecliff Lake is a lake community in West Milford Township, run by the Pinecliff Lake Community Club. Water from the lake feeds Belcher Creek, which is the main inlet to Greenwood Lake.

Homes close to a lake and a creek raise their own questions about basements and drains. This page explains them without guessing about any one house.

Pinecliff Lake in brief

Pinecliff Lake is one of the named lake communities in West Milford, and the township includes it in a watershed study of private lakes funded by the Highlands Council. Belcher Creek originates from the lake and flows to Greenwood Lake.

Membership in the community club controls use of club property. The club is a separate body from the township, and a plumber does not need club membership to work at your house.

Basements and sump pumps

A house near a lake or creek can see groundwater rise after heavy rain or snowmelt. We cannot say that any Pinecliff Lake house has a wet basement, and we do not claim a local flooding history. What we can say is that a working sump pump is a basic defense for any basement.

Test yours before the wet season by pouring water into the pit until the float lifts. If the pump does not start, hums without pumping or cycles constantly, sump pump repair covers a new pump, float switch and discharge line.

Septic context for the township

The Highlands study of the township says 2,210 of 2,392 homes in the Highlands Planning Area, 92 percent, have some type of on-site wastewater system. It also says 86 percent rely on private wells. Those are planning area figures and not a statement about Pinecliff Lake.

When a drain slows, the plumber asks whether the house is on septic and where the tank sits. Drain cleaning handles the house line. A tank that needs pumping is a different job.

If water is coming into the basement

Safety comes first, then the water.

Stay out of deep water

Standing water can hide live electrical hazards. Do not wade in if outlets or the panel are low.

Shut off power if safe

Switch off the breaker from a dry spot, or have the utility disconnect if you cannot reach it safely.

Find the source

Look for clean water from a pipe or groundwater from the walls. The plumber needs to know which.

Call and document

Take photos for insurance, then call the plumber with what you saw.

Water heaters and fixtures

Some lake houses are used in bursts. A water heater that sits through a quiet winter can leak when it restarts, and a faucet that was fine last season may drip after months unused. Water heater repair and faucet repair cover the usual restart problems.

We are not saying every seasonal house has these issues. Ask the plumber to check the heater, the shutoffs and the fixtures when you open a house for the season.

Community rules and township permits

The Pinecliff Lake Community Club sets rules for its own properties and beaches. Those rules do not change plumbing code. New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. For permits, permit rules vary, so ask the West Milford building department at 1480 Union Valley Road.

If your house is close to the lake, tell the plumber. The route for hoses, trucks and equipment may depend on the road and your lot.

Pinecliff Lake plumbing questions

Is Pinecliff Lake in West Milford?

Yes. It is a lake community in West Milford Township, Passaic County, and the township includes it in a study of private lakes.

Does my house need a sump pump?

That depends on the house and the lot. A plumber can look at the basement and tell you whether a pump is advisable.

Do you need club membership to work at my house?

No. The community club governs its own properties. A plumber working in your home does not need membership.

Is my home on septic?

Many township homes are, but we did not find a street-level list. Check your records or ask the township.

Can I get a price before work starts?

Yes. We give a clear price before work starts once the plumber has seen the problem.
