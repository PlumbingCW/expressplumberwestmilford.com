If water is bubbling up in the yard, pressure has collapsed or the house has no water at all, the service line may be broken. Shut off the main valve inside the house and call our 24/7 line.

The service line is the buried pipe that brings water from the street main or from a private well into the house. It runs under lawns, driveways and tree roots, so a break can run unseen for days.

Before digging, a utility mark-out is required. In New Jersey that means calling NJ One Call at 811, which needs at least three full business days of notice, so the plumber plans the dig around that step.

Public water or private well

West Milford is a patchwork. A township groundwater study recorded 2,449 domestic wells, so many homes have a well line running from the wellhead or pressure tank to the house rather than a municipal main connection.

The repair is similar in each case, but the pump and pressure tank matter on a well. A leak in the line makes the pump run constantly, which wears it out, so the plumber checks the pump when the leak is found.

Signs of a broken service line

Some clues are subtle.

Sudden pressure loss

Pressure drops at every fixture without any other cause.

Wet or sunken spot in the yard

Water finds the easiest route to the surface and can wash out soil.

Pump that never stops

On a well, the pump runs because the system never reaches its set pressure.

Rising water bill

On public water, a leaking line makes the meter turn faster.

Frost depth and the long view

Water lines must sit below the frost line so they do not freeze. A shallow or exposed section can freeze in a cold snap and split, and a repair is a chance to correct the depth.

If you are not sure where the line is breaking, leak detection narrows the search before anyone digs.

What to do while you wait

Shut off the main valve inside the house, keep vehicles off the wet spot in the yard and avoid digging yourself. A buried line can sit near other utilities.

Call NJ One Call at 811 only if the plumber asks you to arrange a mark-out for planned digging.

Water service line questions

Do you handle water service line repair emergencies at night or on weekends in West Milford?

Yes. A broken service line is an emergency and the 24/7 line takes those calls nights, weekends and holidays. Shut off the main valve and call.

Who owns the service line?

On public water, the owner is usually responsible from the meter or curb stop to the house. The plumber can help you tell where that point is.

How do I find the line?

The plumber uses the mark-out from NJ One Call and, if needed, a locator to trace the pipe.

Do I need a permit?

Some service line work needs one. The plumber checks with the township's Department of Building Safety.

Can the line be replaced without a trench?

Sometimes. A trenchless method depends on the pipe, the soil and the route.

What if my well pump keeps running?

A leak anywhere downstream can cause that. The plumber checks the line and the pressure system together.

What rules apply to digging for a service line?

Digging requires a utility mark-out through NJ One Call at 811, and the pipe work follows the National Standard Plumbing Code adopted in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15.
