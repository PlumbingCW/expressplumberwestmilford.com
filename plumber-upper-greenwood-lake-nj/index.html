Upper Greenwood Lake is a lake community in West Milford Township, built around a man-made lake of about 400 acres. It is separate from the larger Greenwood Lake that crosses the New York line, and people often mix the two up.

Many homes here began as summer cottages and later became full-time houses. That shapes the plumbing questions we hear, so this page starts there.

Upper Greenwood Lake in brief

The community sits on Bearfort Ridge at about 1,135 feet. Mirror Holding Company bought roughly 1,500 acres in 1932 and sold numbered lots through the New York Daily Mirror. The Upper Greenwood Lake Property Owners Association formed in 1933.

The lake has about 10.5 miles of shoreline. The area was once called Moe, after a landowner, and the community was electrified in 1936.

From seasonal cottage to year-round home

The association history says many homes were converted to year-round use after World War II. A house that began as a seasonal cottage may have had its water, heating and drains upgraded in stages. We do not know which houses had which changes, so the plumber looks at the system as it is.

The practical point is simple. A year-round house needs water lines that survive winter and a drain system that handles daily use. If you bought a house that used to be a cottage, ask what has been replaced and keep the answer.

Leaks you cannot see

A small supply leak can run for weeks in a crawl space or behind a wall. Warning signs include a water bill that rises with no change in use, a meter that moves when everything is off, or a damp smell with no wet spot. A leak detection visit uses sound and pressure tools to find the line without tearing out finished walls.

If the leak is on the line between the street or well and the house, water service line repair replaces the buried section. Your plumber will explain which part of the line is yours before work starts.

Signs of a hidden leak

None of these prove a leak. Together they are a reason to call.

Rising bill

Usage climbs with no change in habits.

Moving meter

The meter dial turns when every tap and appliance is off.

Musty smell

A damp odor near a wall, floor or cabinet with no visible source.

Soft floor

Flooring or drywall feels spongy or has bubbled paint.

Freezing at 1,135 feet

Upper Greenwood Lake is at a higher elevation than most of the township, so winter nights can be hard on pipes. For a house that sits empty for part of the year, the safest habit is to shut off the main and drain the lines before leaving, then restart carefully on return.

If a pipe splits, burst pipe repair covers finding the damage, replacing the section and testing the line. Do not wait for the thaw. A frozen pipe can hide a split that only leaks once it warms.

Drains and septic

The township groundwater study found that about 72 percent of township soils have severe septic limitations, and the township Open Space Plan describes homes on this plateau as scattered through the landscape. We did not find a source on sewer or septic for Upper Greenwood Lake, so we do not state one.

On a drain call the plumber asks first about the system. A single slow fixture points to a local clog, and drain cleaning often fixes it. Several slow fixtures together point to the main line or the tank.

Know who is responsible

The property owners association owns streets, parkland and the clubhouse, according to its own history. That does not mean it handles your plumbing. Water and drain lines inside your lot are normally yours.

If your lot has lake access rules or work near the shoreline, check with the association before starting. For permits, permit rules vary, so ask the West Milford building department at 1480 Union Valley Road. New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15.

Upper Greenwood Lake plumbing questions

Is Upper Greenwood Lake the same as Greenwood Lake?

No. Upper Greenwood Lake is a separate, private lake community in West Milford. Greenwood Lake is a larger lake of about 1,920 acres that crosses the New York line.

What ZIP code covers Upper Greenwood Lake?

Several ZIP codes touch the township, including 07480, 07421 and 07435. Check your own mail for yours.

Is my cottage conversion safe for winter?

A plumber can check exposed lines and recommend insulation or rerouting. We do not assume what any house has.

Does the association handle plumbing?

Its history says it owns the streets, parkland and clubhouse. Plumbing inside your lot is normally the owner's responsibility.

Can you find a leak without opening the wall?

Often yes. Leak detection uses sound and pressure tools, and the plumber explains what is needed before work starts.
