If water is on the floor of a shop, restaurant or office, shut off the nearest isolation valve, protect the stock and electronics you can and call our 24/7 line. The sooner the water is stopped the smaller the cleanup and the shorter the closure.

Commercial plumbing differs from residential work in scale and in consequences. A clogged line can stop a kitchen, a failed heater can close a salon and a burst supply pipe can ruin inventory in a night.

We focus on the problems that stop a business from operating, and we work out a plan with the manager or owner so that repairs fit around opening hours where possible.

Emergency problems we handle for local businesses

Typical calls include main line blockages, floor drain backups, restroom fixture failures, leaking supply lines and water heater problems. Smaller shops along Union Valley Road and the Route 23 and County Road 511 corridors tend to have older piping behind finished walls, which can make leaks hard to see.

We also help property owners who manage several units, where one failure can affect several tenants at once.

Kitchens, grease and drains

Restaurants and cafes have a unique drain problem, which is grease. Warm grease runs freely and then cools and hardens on pipe walls, so a kitchen line that moves fine in the morning can be nearly closed by Friday night.

Regular cleaning through drain cleaning and a kitchen routine that keeps grease out of the sink go further than waiting for a failure.

Know your shutoffs before you need them

Every business should know where the main shutoff valve is and where each isolation valve sits. Label them, test them once a year and make sure staff on every shift can find them. A valve that is seized or buried behind stock is just as useless as no valve at all.

Backflow and water heater checks at commercial sites

Some commercial properties have backflow preventers on the water supply that need periodic testing, and the rules are set by the water provider that serves the building. Ask your provider whether yours is due.

Water heaters at a shop or office often sit in a back room with stored goods piled around them. Keep the area clear, because a leaking tank hidden behind stock can flood a stockroom before anyone sees it.

Commercial plumbing questions

Do you handle commercial plumbing emergencies at night or on weekends in West Milford?

Yes. The 24/7 line handles commercial calls nights, weekends and holidays. Shut off the nearest valve, protect what you can and call.

Do you work on restaurants?

Yes, including kitchen drains, grease-related backups, dishwasher lines and water heaters.

Can you work after business hours?

We can schedule work around your hours where the problem allows it. Emergency calls are handled any time.

Do you handle multi-unit buildings?

Yes. The plumber isolates the failure so other units are affected as little as possible.

Are permits required for commercial work?

Some jobs are covered by the plumbing subcode and need a permit. The plumber confirms during the visit.

What should I have ready when I call?

The building address, what you have shut off and where water is showing up.

What standards apply to commercial plumbing?

Commercial work follows the state plumbing subcode and the National Standard Plumbing Code adopted in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and some jobs need a permit and inspection.
