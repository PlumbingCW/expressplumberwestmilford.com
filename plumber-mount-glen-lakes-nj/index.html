Mount Glen Lakes is a lake community in West Milford Township with an Upper Lake and a Lower Lake. The association runs the community, and its own history lists services such as water, sewage and parks among its purposes.

That raises a practical question for every owner. Which parts of your plumbing belong to you, and which belong to the association? This page helps you ask it.

Mount Glen Lakes in brief

The association history says Dudley and Clara Shippee developed the community and sold lots in the early 1930s. The association incorporated in 1937, and in 1952 the remaining land passed to L.C. Larsen Construction Company, subject to lake-use privileges.

The association publishes a page about Lower Lake dam updates, which is the kind of community matter a resident should follow. It does not affect your indoor plumbing.

Ask who owns the pipe

The association history lists water, sewage and parks among its purposes. We did not find a public statement of which services it provides today, so we do not say. Ask the association whether it supplies water, manages shared lines or leaves everything to individual owners.

The answer decides who you call for a main line problem. A plumber can repair the pipe that serves your house. A shared line may be the association's responsibility. Knowing which saves a wasted visit.

Drains that keep coming back

A cable clears a clog. It does not always remove the build-up that caused it. For grease or scale in a house line, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water, and a camera shows what is left.

If a drain slows again soon after cabling, ask for a camera look before paying for another cable run. The picture tells you whether you have a clog, a belly in the pipe or a damaged section.

Before the plumber arrives

A few steps make the visit faster.

Locate the cleanout

A capped pipe near the foundation is often the cleanout. Clear the area around it.

Note which fixtures are slow

One fixture points to a local clog. Several point to the main line.

Keep pets away

Plumbers work with cables and hoses, and a calm space is safer for everyone.

Write down the history

When did it start, and has it happened before? The plumber uses that to decide where to look.

Toilets, fixtures and water heaters

A house in any older community may have fixtures from different decades. We do not assume what any one house has. A running toilet usually points to a worn flapper or fill valve, which toilet repair handles in one visit.

A tank water heater that leaks at the base is usually at the end of its life, and water heater replacement covers the right size and venting. A heater that only runs cool may be a thermostat or element problem.

Permits and licensing

New Jersey allows plumbing work only by a master plumber, an authorized contractor or their employees. Permit rules vary, so ask the West Milford building department at 1480 Union Valley Road, and check any plumber with the Division of Consumer Affairs before you hire.

Association rules cover lake use and community property. They do not replace township permits or the National Standard Plumbing Code that New Jersey adopts through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15.
