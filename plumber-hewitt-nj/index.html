Hewitt in brief

Hewitt is an unincorporated community in West Milford Township and is named for Abram Hewitt. Its elevation is about 430 feet. The 2010 Census counted 7,439 residents in ZIP code area 07421, which is larger than the community itself.

Treat that number as a rough guide to size. It is a ZIP code count and not a head count for the neighborhood.

Water source comes first

The township groundwater study says groundwater is the primary drinking water source and records 2,449 domestic wells. The median well depth is 163 feet and the median yield is 7 gallons per minute. We have not found a source that says which Hewitt streets use wells, so the plumber asks at the start of the call.

A house on a well has a pump, a pressure tank and often a treatment device. Weak pressure, sputtering faucets or a pump that cycles often can start at the pump or tank rather than in the house piping. A house on public water starts with the meter and the main shutoff.

Water heaters in single-family houses

ZIP code housing data describes Hewitt as largely single-family housing, so the water heater is usually in a basement, utility room or garage. A tank that leaks at the base is usually near the end of its life. A unit that makes no hot water may only need a thermostat, element or igniter.

Our plumber checks the cause before recommending a new unit. If repair makes sense, water heater repair is the shorter visit. If the tank is failing, water heater replacement covers the right size, venting and the permit question.

Before you call about a leak

These four steps limit damage while you wait for the plumber.

Find the main shutoff

Turn the main water valve to off. If the house is on a well, switch off the pump breaker as well.

Keep power away from water

Do not touch outlets or appliances standing in water. Turn off the breaker for that area if you can reach it dry.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and furniture off the wet floor so the plumber can see where the water starts.

Take photos

Photograph the damage before cleanup. Photos help with the repair estimate and any insurance claim.

Drains and the septic question

The same groundwater study found that about 72 percent of township soils have severe limitations for septic systems and about 2 percent are suitable for conventional ones. That does not tell us what any one Hewitt house has. It does mean the first question on a drain call is whether the house is on a septic system.

If a septic tank is full, cabling the house line will not help. If the problem is a grease or scale build-up in the house line, hydro jetting or a cable can clear it. The plumber tells you which case it is before work starts.

Permits and licensing

New Jersey allows plumbing work only by a master plumber, an authorized contractor or their employees. The state adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Permit rules vary, so ask the West Milford building department, which is at 1480 Union Valley Road.

You can check any plumber with the Division of Consumer Affairs license verification system before hiring. We would rather you check than guess.
