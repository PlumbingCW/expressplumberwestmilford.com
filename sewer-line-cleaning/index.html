A main sewer line that is full of water is an emergency, especially when it reaches a basement floor drain. Call the 24/7 line, stop using toilets and sinks and keep water out of the system until the line is open.

The main sewer line carries everything from the house to the street connection or the septic tank, so one stoppage shows up everywhere at once. Unlike a sink clog, it cannot be solved with a plunger or a store-bought liquid.

We work from the main cleanout, clear the blockage and then look at why it formed, because the same roots or the same belly in the pipe will bring the problem back.

Emergency first step: finding the main cleanout

Most houses have a cleanout near where the sewer line leaves the foundation, or a capped fitting in the basement or yard. If you do not know where yours is, the plumber will find it, and you can ask us to mark it for the future.

A clear cleanout is the difference between a quick visit and a long one. If none exists, installing one is often the best money spent on the line.

What a blocked main looks like

The warning signs usually come in a pattern.

Toilets bubble when the sink drains

Air displaced by water is trapped behind the blockage and escapes through the nearest trap.

Lowest drain backs up first

The basement drain or tub overflows before upstairs fixtures show anything.

Sewage smell near the cleanout

Water standing against a blockage lets gases escape near the access point.

Slow drains throughout the house

No one fixture is the problem. The shared line is.

Whole house draining slowly?

Call the 24/7 line before the next load of laundry. A full main line can push wastewater into the basement.

What is usually in the line

In older neighborhoods the cause is often roots in clay or cast iron pipe. In newer houses it is more often paper, wipes and grease than roots.

A cable clears a fresh stoppage. If the line has a heavy coating of grease or scale, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall more thoroughly.

Sewer line cleaning questions

Do you handle sewer line cleaning emergencies at night or on weekends in West Milford?

Yes. A backing-up main line is an emergency, and the 24/7 line takes calls nights, weekends and holidays. Stop water use and call right away.

How often should a sewer line be cleaned?

Only when it shows symptoms. Homes with roots or repeat clogs may benefit from a camera look and a plan, not a calendar.

Can I clear a main line myself?

Rental machines can injure people and damage pipe. A plunger and a store drain opener rarely help once the main is full.

Why does my line keep clogging?

Roots, a belly that holds water, grease or an offset joint cause repeats. A camera inspection finds out which.

Will cleaning fix a cracked pipe?

No. Cleaning clears the blockage but a crack needs repair.

Does septic change the plan?

Yes. If the blockage is at the tank or the field, the line cleaning is only part of the answer, and the plumber tells you so.

Is there a code for cleaning a sewer line?

Cleaning is maintenance, but a repair that follows must meet the National Standard Plumbing Code that New Jersey adopts in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15.
